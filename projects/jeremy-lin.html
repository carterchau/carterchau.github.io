Jeremy Lin: Evaluating Journalism’s Coverage of Basketball’s Asian American Star

How commercialization, sensationalism, and racial framing turned Jeremy Lin’s 2012 run with the New York Knicks into a cultural phenomenon.

Read Full Research Paper ↗← All Projects

On February 4, 2012, Jeremy Lin entered a Knicks game against the New Jersey Nets and sparked what would become known as “Linsanity.” This paper revisits that run through the lens of media history, examining the narratives that turned his basketball performance into a larger cultural story.

Commercialization

As Lin’s popularity spread, news organizations highlighted his underdog background and unexpected success. The paper connects the demand for these human-interest narratives to longer histories of commercially driven journalism.

Sensationalism

Coverage of Lin’s matchup with Kobe Bryant demonstrates how sports reporting can manufacture anticipation and rivalry. The resulting headlines became part of the story surrounding Lin’s 38-point performance against the Lakers.

Racial Framing

Coverage of Lin’s Taiwanese American identity created opportunities for representation and identification among Asian American journalists and fans, while also exposing stereotypes, microaggressions, and overt racism in sports media.
