LeBron James: NBA All-Time Scoring Leader, Olympic Gold Medalist, and Barbie Doll?

By Carter Chau · 452 words

On April 14, toy store shelves were packed with the new “Kenbassador” dolls. According to Barbie's manufacturing company, Mattel, this line of figures celebrates influential male figures creating positive change in the world and their communities. LeBron James was the first doll in the series.

“I'd pay a million dollars for it. Seventy-five dollars is a steal, in my opinion.” — Nigel Swinson

The LeBron James doll sold for $75 across stores such as Target and Walmart. Theater first-year Cassidey Fralin said the cost of the figurine is “unfair,” arguing that pricing it as a collectible works against Barbie's appeal as a toy families can play with and share.

Fourth-year student Dhruv Patel called the toy comical, referencing James' popularity in memes on his TikTok “For You” page.

Weinberg first-year Saniya Newman said the collaboration could also push Barbie beyond its association with young girls. “Men should have the freedom like girls to play with either toy without gender norms,” Newman said. “So this could be a step into the future.”

Theater first-year Mariah Waters, who grew up with Barbie dolls, said the figurine could inspire boys like her brother to play with the toys. Weinberg fourth-year Namish Kaistha called the collaboration a strong move for representation and an opportunity for Barbie to reach the youth basketball market.

The LeBron James Kenbassador has since sold out and appeared on resale websites for more than $100. First-year Tamyrha Dunoc said she was not surprised, pointing to James' loyal fan base and the interest from both children and adult collectors.
